A floral embroidery pattern PDF, explained

Each pattern is one PDF you print at home. Nothing here links to a file, because the files are not finished and the shop is not open.

  • Line art in three hoop sizes

    The same drawing prepared for 4-inch, 6-inch, and 8-inch hoops, so you print the size you mean to stitch.

  • A stitch guide with diagrams

    Step drawings for stem stitch, satin stitch, and French knots, plus backstitch and lazy daisy where the motif uses them.

  • A DMC color list

    Cotton floss numbers and how many strands to use. The numbers name the colors; they are not a logo.

  • A supply list

    Hoop, fabric, needle, and a way to transfer the lines. Ordinary tools, written out so a first project is not a scavenger hunt.

  • A photo of the finished piece

    Added once we have stitched the sample. That photograph is not on this page, because the sample is not done.

  • US Letter pages

    Laid out for 8.5 by 11 inch paper. An A4 file is not part of this plan.

Beginner hand embroidery patterns, with flowers

Garden Sprig is the pattern to start with if you have not stitched a flower before. The three stitches below are the ones the guides will teach. These drawings are a plain version of the idea, not a page from the PDF, which is still being drawn.

Stem stitch

A rope of overlapping stitches for stems and outlines.

  1. Bring the needle up at the start of the line.
  2. Take a short stitch ahead, and come up halfway back, to one side of the line.
  3. Repeat, keeping the working thread on the same side.

Satin stitch

Straight stitches laid side by side to fill a petal or a leaf.

  1. Mark the shape lightly, or trust the transferred line.
  2. Lay stitches from edge to edge so they touch.
  3. Keep the edges even. Do not pile thread on thread.

French knot

A wrapped dot for flower centers and small buds.

  1. Bring the needle up where the dot should sit.
  2. Wrap the thread once or twice around the needle.
  3. Go back down just beside the start, holding the wraps, and pull through slowly.

For these small florals, two strands of six-strand floss are the usual weight. The PDF will say if a motif wants one strand or three. Fabric is cotton or linen, not Aida. Aida is for counted cross-stitch, which this line is not.

Supplies to have ready

Buy these when you are ready to stitch. This page does not link to a shop.

  • Hoop, 4, 6, or 8 inch, to match the line art you print.
  • Cotton or linen, washed and pressed, large enough to sit in the hoop with a margin.
  • Embroidery needle, about size 7 to 9.
  • Six-strand cotton floss in the DMC numbers the PDF lists.
  • Small sharp scissors and a water-soluble pen, or a bright window for tracing.
  • Optional: a light stabilizer if the fabric is very fine.

How you will use the PDF

  1. 1

    Print the line art at the hoop size you are stitching, on US Letter paper.

  2. 2

    Transfer the lines. Tape the page to a window, lay the fabric over it, and trace. A light pad works the same way.

  3. 3

    Hoop the fabric so it is firm, not drum-tight. The inner ring sits under the cloth.

  4. 4

    Stitch from the guide, using the color list for the floss.

  5. 5

    Finish the back and leave the hoop as the frame, or move the cloth to another one.

Questions

Is a floral embroidery pattern PDF the same as cross-stitch?

No. These are hand embroidery patterns, also called surface embroidery. You follow a printed line on plain cotton or linen. Cross-stitch is counted on a grid. EverBloom's finished cross-stitch hoops are a separate collection, not a downloadable chart.

What will be in the PDF?

Printable line art for 4-inch, 6-inch, and 8-inch hoops, a stitch guide with step diagrams, a DMC cotton-floss color list, a supply list, and a photograph of the finished stitching once the sample hoop is complete. Pages are planned for US Letter paper.

Is this a beginner hand embroidery pattern for flowers?

Yes. Garden Sprig is planned as a first project: one stem, leaves, and blossoms in stem stitch, satin stitch, and French knots. The guide will teach those stitches. It does not assume you already know them.
