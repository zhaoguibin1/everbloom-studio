Stitches these flowers use

Wildflower Meadow is worked in stem stitch, satin stitch, lazy daisy, French knots, and backstitch. The three drawings below are a plain version of stitches the guides teach. They are not a page from the PDF. The PDF’s own stitch library is in the file.

Stem stitch

A rope of overlapping stitches for stems and outlines.

  1. Bring the needle up at the start of the line.
  2. Take a short stitch ahead, and come up halfway back, to one side of the line.
  3. Repeat, keeping the working thread on the same side.

Satin stitch

Straight stitches laid side by side to fill a petal or a leaf.

  1. Mark the shape lightly, or trust the transferred line.
  2. Lay stitches from edge to edge so they touch.
  3. Keep the edges even. Do not pile thread on thread.

French knot

A wrapped dot for flower centers and small buds.

  1. Bring the needle up where the dot should sit.
  2. Wrap the thread once or twice around the needle.
  3. Go back down just beside the start, holding the wraps, and pull through slowly.

For these small florals, two strands of six-strand floss are the usual weight. The PDF says where a motif wants one strand or three. Fabric is cotton or linen, not Aida. Aida is for counted cross-stitch, which this line is not.

Supplies to have ready

Buy these when you are ready to stitch. This page does not link to a shop.

  • Hoop, 5, 6, or 7 inch, to match the pattern you are stitching.
  • Cotton or linen, washed and pressed, large enough to sit in the hoop with a margin.
  • Embroidery needle, about size 7 to 9.
  • Six-strand cotton floss in the DMC numbers the PDF lists.
  • Small sharp scissors and a water-soluble pen, or a bright window for tracing.

How you use the PDF

  1. 1

    Print the transfer at 100% and measure the 1-inch test square. It should be 1 inch, not “close enough.”

  2. 2

    Transfer the lines. Tape the page to a window, lay the fabric over it, and trace. A light pad works the same way.

  3. 3

    Hoop the fabric so it is firm, not drum-tight. The inner ring sits under the cloth.

  4. 4

    Stitch from the guide, using the color list for the floss.

  5. 5

    Finish the back and leave the hoop as the frame, or move the cloth to another one.

Questions

Is a floral embroidery pattern PDF the same as cross-stitch?

No. These are hand embroidery patterns, also called surface embroidery. You follow a printed line on plain cotton or linen. Cross-stitch is counted on a grid. The counted charts are a separate pattern line.

What is in the PDF?

An actual-size transfer, a mirrored transfer, a color and stitch guide, a stitch library, and a DMC floss list. Print at 100% and check the 1-inch test square. The sample images on this page are watermarked. The file we send is the full pattern.
